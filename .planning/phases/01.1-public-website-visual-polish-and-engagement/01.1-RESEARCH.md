## User Constraints (from CONTEXT.md)

### Locked Decisions

### Visual character and typography
- **D-01:** Use a premium industrial visual direction that feels modern, commercially serious, trustworthy, and appropriate for the automotive/truck market.
- **D-02:** The approved Hino logo is the primary header and footer brand. `Cebu` is a secondary location descriptor and must never substitute for or visually overpower the Hino mark.
- **D-03:** Use geometric premium typography with a contemporary automotive-showroom character. Maintain high body-copy legibility, strong hierarchy, a lean font payload, and only the weights the interface needs.
- **D-04:** Cards and panels are crisp and minimal: subtle rounding, thin borders, restrained shadows, generous whitespace, and photography carrying most of the visual weight.
- **D-05:** Keep pages predominantly white and spacious. Reserve charcoal for the hero, footer, and selected emphasis sections; use Hino red as a controlled accent and conversion signal.

### Header, navigation, and motion
- **D-06:** Remove the upper phone utility strip. Phone access remains available in the mobile sticky actions, contact page, footer, and mobile menu.
- **D-07:** Use concise, task-first desktop navigation: `Trucks`, `Find Your Truck`, `Parts`, `Service`, `Hino Cebu`, and a prominent `Get a Quote` action. Secondary destinations remain discoverable through relevant pages and the footer.
- **D-08:** Show the current location with a persistent Hino-red underline. Use a subtle neutral hover background and a clearly visible keyboard-focus outline; state must never rely on color alone.
- **D-09:** Use restrained 180-220 ms color, underline, and menu open/close micro-transitions. Do not add full-page transitions, a general animation framework, or motion that survives `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Photography and asset placement
- **D-10:** Until an approved Cebu-specific hero is available, lead the homepage with one strong approved official Hino truck image without implying that it depicts Cebu inventory.
- **D-11:** Use purposeful imagery at major decision points: truck cards, service, parts, financing, branch/location, and major page heroes. Keep supporting informational sections clean and text-led.
- **D-12:** Where Cebu-specific photography is unavailable, use the closest approved official Hino Philippines asset only when the presentation cannot be mistaken for Cebu inventory, facilities, staff, customers, or local availability.
- **D-13:** Apply a local-first replacement rule by subject. Approved Cebu branch, service, parts, staff, and delivery photography replaces equivalent national fallback imagery; official product imagery remains when it is the clearest model representation.
- **D-14:** Every image remains subject to the existing source, authorization, Cebu-applicability, alt-text, responsive sizing, and fixed-dimension controls.

### Social presence and footer
- **D-15:** Place Facebook, YouTube, and Instagram shells in the footer only; the header remains focused on customer tasks and conversion.
- **D-16:** Before profile URLs are verified, render muted, non-clickable platform icons with concise copy explaining that official Hino Cebu profiles will be added after verification. Do not create dead links or temporarily link to national profiles.
- **D-17:** Once approved, social links open in a new browser tab and receive accessible external-link labeling plus safe external-link attributes.
- **D-18:** Use a conversion-focused multi-column footer containing the Hino logo and Cebu identity, approved branch contact details and hours, key customer paths, a Get a Quote action, social row, and legal links.

- Select the exact authorized or open-licensed geometric font family, fallback stack, weight set, and responsive type scale while honoring D-03 and the performance budget.
- Select exact spacing, border, shadow, and transition token values within the locked visual direction.
- Choose an accessible icon source and precise footer column behavior without adding a heavy component or icon library.
- Choose responsive image crops and art direction within the approved source and non-misrepresentation rules.

## Summary

Implement this phase as a refinement of the existing public shell and shared presentation layer, not as a redesign or dependency expansion. The approved UI contract already fixes the font, token values, breakpoints, component ownership, copy, image subjects, motion limits, and responsive layouts. The planner should sequence the work from safe DTO projections, to the shared shell, to typography/tokens, to shared hero/card/media primitives, then route-level placement and verification. [VERIFIED: `01.1-CONTEXT.md`; `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; codebase grep]

The highest-risk integration is eligibility, not CSS. `src/app/(public)/layout.tsx` currently derives navigation from `getEligibleRoutes()` but labels only `ROUTE-TRUCKS`; the governed route catalog includes Parts, Service, Quote, and other routes but has no dedicated records for `/find-your-truck` or `/hino-cebu`. The desired six-item navigation therefore cannot be produced by adding hardcoded links. The plan must define a documented, fail-closed projection for those two destinations from already eligible data, or omit them until such a projection exists. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

The technical implementation needs no new package. Next.js already provides build-time self-hosting for Google fonts and responsive image optimization; current Next.js 16 documentation deprecates `Image priority` in favor of `preload`. Use Barlow 400/700 once at the root layout, update only the homepage LCP image to `preload`, leave below-fold media lazy by default, and remove `priority` from non-LCP detail images. Use Node built-ins to enforce explicit gzip/source budgets after `next build`. Refactor the existing MarketingTags owner into a first-party, strict versioned consent gate so provider scripts and requests remain absent on missing, malformed, denied, or purpose-insufficient consent; do not build the Phase 3 consent UI or tag-policy workflow here. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/font; CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image; VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**Primary recommendation:** Plan a governance-first, zero-new-dependency polish pass that extends the existing public-shell DTOs and shared CSS/component contracts, then verifies every state at 320px, 720px, 1060px, and desktop without weakening Phase 1 omission rules. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; codebase grep]

### Pattern 1: Server-produced shell DTOs

**What:** Resolve eligibility in `src/app/(public)/layout.tsx`, project only stable IDs/labels/hrefs/statuses, and pass those safe values to client/server components. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**When to use:** Navigation, contact actions, footer details, and social profiles. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

```typescript
// Source: existing public layout + Phase 1 fail-closed selector pattern
const navigation = getEligibleRoutes().flatMap((route) => {
  const label = navigationLabels[route.routeId];
  if (route.status === "withheld" || !label) return [];
  return [{ navigationId: route.routeId, label, href: route.path }];
});
```

Do not make `Header` or another client component import `siteConfig`, governance records, or eligibility selectors. [VERIFIED: `tests/governance.test.mjs`]

### Pattern 2: One global font instance

**What:** Declare Barlow once at the root and expose it as a CSS variable or class. Next.js downloads Google-font CSS/files at build time and serves them from the deployment origin. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/font]

```typescript
// Source: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/font
import { Barlow } from "next/font/google";

const barlow = Barlow({
  subsets: ["latin"],
  weight: ["400", "700"],
  style: ["normal"],
  display: "swap",
  fallback: ["Arial", "sans-serif"],
  variable: "--font-barlow",
});
```

Apply the variable/class at the single root layout, then use it through `font-family: var(--font-barlow), Arial, sans-serif`; do not instantiate the same font in multiple modules. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/font]

### Pattern 3: Responsive image slot with stable geometry

**What:** The server selects an eligible source; the component reserves the intended ratio and lets `next/image` generate a responsive `srcset`. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image]

```tsx
// Source: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image
<div className="home-hero-image">
  <Image
    src="/images/official/hino-300.jpg"
    alt="Hino 300 Series truck"
    fill
    preload
    sizes="(max-width: 719px) 100vw, (max-width: 1059px) 100vw, 58vw"
  />
</div>
```

Only the homepage hero should preload. Width/height or a `fill` container reserves aspect ratio and reduces layout shift; responsive `sizes` prevents the browser from assuming `100vw` unnecessarily. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image]

### Pattern 4: Disclosure navigation, not ARIA menu

**What:** Keep the mobile site navigation as a native button controlling a normal navigation region and ordinary links. Update `aria-expanded`, close on Escape, and return focus to the trigger. [CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/]

**When to use:** The sub-1060px Header. Do not add `role="menu"`/`menuitem` or a focus trap; those patterns impose desktop-application keyboard behavior that this ordinary site navigation does not need. [CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/; CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/menu-button/]

### Pattern 5: CSS-only motion with immediate reduced-motion state

**What:** Animate only allowed paint/composite properties for 200ms/ease-out and globally remove transitions/animations under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html]

**When to use:** Underline, colors, card lift, and mobile panel opacity/maximum 8px translation. Hidden mobile links must not remain keyboard-focusable; use a visibility/display/state strategy that matches the transition. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Hardcoded desired navigation:** It would expose routes outside the canonical eligibility boundary; derive or omit every item. [VERIFIED: Phase 1 D-05 through D-08; codebase grep]
- **Using `siteConfig.socials`:** It currently contains national Hino Motors Philippines URLs, which D-16 explicitly forbids as temporary Hino Cebu destinations. [VERIFIED: codebase grep; `01.1-CONTEXT.md`]
- **Animating `display: none`:** `display` cannot provide the requested enter/exit micro-transition; design the closed state so it is visually hidden and removed from focus while opacity/transform transitions remain possible. [ASSUMED]
- **Treating all cards as interactive:** Lift, pointer cursor, and linked-card focus treatment belong only on a card whose full surface is actually a link. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]
- **Using route-local raw media paths:** This loses provenance and replacement semantics; keep subject-specific asset decisions in typed content/DTOs. [VERIFIED: `01.1-CONTEXT.md`; `ASSET_REQUIREMENTS.md`]
- **Replacing branch placeholders with national facility imagery:** Branch/location accepts only approved Cebu photography; otherwise render a structured text/contact panel. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]
- **Claiming WCAG completion from source checks:** QUAL-04 requires automated and documented human evidence; a regex/source contract alone cannot prove contrast, keyboard, screen-reader, responsive, or touch behavior. [VERIFIED: `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`]

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Desired navigation bypasses route eligibility

**What goes wrong:** The planner adds all six labels from D-07 directly to Header props, exposing a route even when its truth contract is withheld. [VERIFIED: codebase grep; Phase 1 D-07]

**Why it happens:** `/find-your-truck` and `/hino-cebu` exist as files but do not have dedicated entries in the current governed route catalog. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** First define the lawful projection for each destination from existing eligible sources; otherwise omit it with no gap. Never read `siteConfig.nav` from the public shell. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; `tests/governance.test.mjs`]

**Warning signs:** Literal public hrefs in Header/Footer/StickyMobileActions, client imports from governance/content modules, or tests weakened to permit hardcoded paths. [VERIFIED: `tests/governance.test.mjs`]

### Pitfall 2: Active state works only on exact index routes

**What goes wrong:** `/trucks/hino-300` does not mark `Trucks` current because the existing Header compares `pathname === item.href`. [VERIFIED: codebase grep]

**How to avoid:** Use a root-safe segment matcher such as exact match or `pathname.startsWith(item.href + "/")`; set `aria-current="page"` on the representing parent destination and preserve the non-color underline. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/]

**Warning signs:** Red underline disappears on model-detail and nested branch pages. [VERIFIED: codebase route structure]

### Pitfall 3: Menu motion breaks keyboard behavior

**What goes wrong:** Closed links stay tabbable, Escape closes without returning focus, or display toggling prevents the required close transition. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; ASSUMED]

**How to avoid:** Keep a trigger ref, install/clean up Escape handling only in the existing Header client boundary, synchronize `aria-expanded`, and make the closed panel both hidden from focus and non-interactive. [CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/]

**Warning signs:** Focus moves behind an apparently closed panel, the trigger cannot be found after Escape, or reduced-motion still translates the panel. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

### Pitfall 4: Deprecated image loading API and too many preloads

**What goes wrong:** Current source uses `priority` on the logo, homepage hero, and truck detail image; Next.js 16 deprecates that prop, and multiple preloads compete for bandwidth. [VERIFIED: codebase grep; CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image]

**How to avoid:** Use `preload` only for the homepage LCP hero, keep other images lazy by default, and verify every `fill` image has a realistic `sizes` value and stable ratio container. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image]

**Warning signs:** Next build warnings about `priority`, detail images loading before the viewport, or mobile downloads sized as `100vw` for multi-column cards. [CITED: https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image]

### Pitfall 5: National imagery becomes a local claim

**What goes wrong:** Copy, alt text, captions, or layout identifies a national service facility, financing graphic, staff, or truck image as Cebu inventory or proof. [VERIFIED: `SOURCE_REGISTER.md`; `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

**How to avoid:** Keep product/support descriptions subject-only, retain national-source caveats, and use no image for branch/location until a Cebu asset is eligible. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; `ASSET_REQUIREMENTS.md`]

**Warning signs:** Words such as “our Cebu facility,” “available now,” “local stock,” or person/location names unsupported by eligible claims. [VERIFIED: project content-truth constraints]

### Pitfall 6: Social shell accidentally activates unverified URLs

**What goes wrong:** Existing national profile links are reused, placeholders become disabled buttons/tab stops, or a verified external link lacks a new-tab announcement and safe relation tokens. [VERIFIED: codebase grep; `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

**How to avoid:** Model `unverified`, `verified`, and `withheld` explicitly; emit no `href` in unavailable states; validate approved URLs before projection; use `target="_blank"`, `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, and an accessible name that announces the new tab only for verified links. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; CITED: https://html.spec.whatwg.org/dev/links.html]

**Warning signs:** `siteConfig.socials` reaches Footer directly, muted icons have hover/pointer styles, or an empty/placeholder URL is rendered. [VERIFIED: codebase grep; `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

### Pitfall 7: Sticky mobile bar overlaps content or safe areas

**What goes wrong:** The fixed 66px bar covers page content or sits behind a device home indicator. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

**How to avoid:** Include `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` in bar padding/height and set body bottom padding to the bar plus safe-area inset; let the grid use the actual eligible action count. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

**Warning signs:** Legal/footer links cannot be reached at the bottom, labels clip, or blank action columns remain when a contact action is withheld. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`]

### Pitfall 8: One blue focus outline is not sufficient on every surface

**What goes wrong:** The approved focus blue `#1378D0` has strong contrast against white (4.54:1), light gray (4.12:1), and charcoal (3.88:1), but only 1.14:1 against Hino red `#D71920`. A blue outline placed directly against a red CTA band can therefore become difficult to perceive. [VERIFIED: local WCAG relative-luminance calculation; CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/understanding/non-text-contrast.html]

**How to avoid:** Keep blue as the focus token but add a surface-separating halo/ring for controls on red backgrounds, then visually verify every focus context rather than checking token pairs in isolation. [VERIFIED: `01.1-UI-SPEC.md`; CITED: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/understanding/non-text-contrast.html]

**Warning signs:** The focus indicator disappears on the final red CTA band or any red-accent control. [VERIFIED: local contrast calculation]

function isCurrentPath(pathname: string, href: string) {
  if (href === "/") return pathname === "/";

}
